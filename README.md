# Wage Inequality in the United States: Race, Gender, and Education (1973–2022)

## Project Overview

This project examines how race, gender, and educational attainment intersect to shape wage inequality in the United States. Using data from the Economic Policy Institute (EPI), we analyze trends in median hourly wages from 1973 to 2022, exploring how earnings differ across demographic groups and how these disparities have evolved over time.

The project was completed as the final report for **Data Science 112**.

## Research Question

How have race, gender, and educational attainment influenced median wages in the United States between 1973 and 2022?

## Key Findings

- **Education and earnings:** Workers with higher educational attainment consistently earn higher median wages. However, wage growth has been stronger for workers with bachelor's and advanced degrees than for those with lower levels of education.
- **Gender wage gap:** Men's median wages have remained higher than women's across the study period, although the gap has narrowed over time.
- **Racial wage inequality:** White workers have consistently earned higher median wages than Black and Hispanic workers, highlighting persistent racial disparities.
- **Intersectionality matters:** Wage disparities vary across combinations of race, gender, and education. Higher education is associated with smaller gender wage differences in some groups, but it does not eliminate wage inequality.
- **Uneven progress:** By 2022, women's median wages had moved closer to men's across the racial groups examined, but the degree of convergence differed by race.

## Data and Methods

**Data source:** Economic Policy Institute (EPI), *State of Working America Data Library*, accessed through Kaggle.

**Time period:** 1973–2022

**Population:** U.S. wage and salary workers aged 16 and older, as represented in the dataset.

The analysis focuses on median hourly wages adjusted to 2022 dollars and examines differences across:

- Educational attainment
- Gender
- Race and ethnicity (White, Black, and Hispanic)
- Intersections of gender, race, and education

The project uses data cleaning, transformation, exploratory data analysis, and visualizations to compare wage trends and distributions across groups.
